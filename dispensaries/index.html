For dispensaries

Help shoppers find what they like the smell of, on your own menu.

NOSE puts a small widget on your website. It shows a shopper the in-stock products whose terpene profile, as the lab measured it, is closest to what they have bought from you before, and says which of their purchases it used. Aroma and flavor only: it says nothing about how a product makes anyone feel.

Try the demo

What your shoppers see

Three views, one for each place on your site. The store and products below are a made-up test store; every terpene panel is a real lab report's.

Your shoppers' purchase history never reaches NOSE

Your page hands a shopper's batch IDs to that shopper's own browser, and the widget ranks your menu there. NOSE serves the code, one feed of your catalog that is the same for every visitor, and takes votes that carry nothing about who voted. What NOSE keeps is set out on the privacy page.

The widget asks the shopper first, and reads their purchases only after they say yes.

What your web team does

One script tag for a pinned release, checked by its hash, so your page shows what you tested until you change the tag. Three lines in your Content-Security-Policy. One element where the widget goes. And a catalog upload from your server, with a secret key that never appears on a page.

The integration guide has every step.

What we do first

A coverage report on your menu: how many of your in-stock flower, pre-roll, vape and concentrate batches have a terpene panel NOSE can read, by category and by lab, and the reason for every one it can't. A batch NOSE can't read shows no panel and no score. Nothing is estimated.

Compliance

Every word the widget shows is in one file you can review, with Florida wording alongside the default. A compliance kit gives you each screen as a picture, with its words and the data it shows, to send to your regulator. NOSE never changes a release you've tested; moving to a new one is your choice.

The pilot

One store. Half your signed-in shoppers see the widget and half see your usual menu, by a random draw kept on your side. One measure decides it: inhalable orders per shopper. The plan is written down before day one, read once at the end, and published whatever it shows, with nothing about any shopper in it.

What NOSE won't do

Say anything about how a product makes someone feel. Guess a profile from a strain name. Score a batch without a lab report it could read. The match score is a hypothesis the pilot exists to test, and the widget says so under its matches.
