Intensity versus character

Two jars can smell like the same thing at different volumes, or like different things at the same volume. A terpene report carries both pieces of information, and NOSE keeps them apart on purpose.

  • 5 min read
  • 3 sources
  • Last reviewed September 2026

In short

  • The total terpene figure is a rough guide to how loud a jar is. The mix, meaning each terpene’s share of that total, describes what it smells like.
  • NOSE scores matches on the mix alone, so a louder jar with the same proportions still scores 100.
  • When you build a palate from several jars, each jar gets an equal say however loud it is.
  • Loudness is shown separately as intensity. It’s useful, but only as a rough guide.
  • Both numbers describe the jar on test day. Terpenes keep evaporating afterwards.

Two questions, one table

Every terpene table answers two different questions. How much aroma material is there? That’s the total. What kind of aroma is it? That’s the balance between the compounds. It’s easy to blur the two, because both come from the same column of numbers.

Here are three jars. The values are made up to keep the arithmetic easy, but the scores are exactly what NOSE’s matcher returns for them.

The mix did.

With only four measured compounds, NOSE would also label both comparisons low confidence. Most real reports list more.

Why NOSE compares shares

Converting each value to its share of the report’s own total takes loudness out of the comparison, so the score answers one question: is the character alike? It matters most when a palate is built from several jars you liked.

Say you liked a quiet jar at 0.80% total terpenes and a loud one at 3.20%, with different characters. NOSE averages their shares, so each jar has an equal say, and the resulting palate scores 85 against the quiet jar and 86 against the loud one. Average the raw numbers instead and the loud jar outvotes the quiet one four to one. That palate would score 98 against the loud jar and only 63 against the quiet one: your palate would quietly turn into a copy of your loudest jar.

What intensity is good for

Loudness still matters; it answers a different question. Whenever NOSE reads a report, it shows the total terpene figure separately, labeled “intensity, not shape.” Read the two together:

  • A strong match with a much lower total suggests a quieter version of a character you like.
  • A strong match with a much higher total suggests the same character, louder.
  • Two jars with similar totals can still be nothing alike, as Jar C shows.

Why intensity is only a rough guide

Treat the total as a hint, not a measurement of how strongly a jar will smell.

  • Noses aren’t scales. The most abundant compounds aren’t always the ones you notice. In a 2021 study of cannabis’s classic skunky note, terpenoids were the most abundant aroma compounds, but the most pungent sample was the one with the most volatile sulfur compounds, a family that standard panels don’t measure (Oswald et al., 2021).
  • Panels only add up what they measured. A report that tests fewer compounds prints a smaller total, even if the jar isn’t any quieter.
  • Some distinctive aromas aren’t driven by terpenes. A 2023 study found terpene profiles remarkably similar across cultivars with very different aromas, and found that their sweet or savory differences tracked minor, non-terpene compounds instead (Oswald et al., 2023). That limits the character side of the comparison too, and it’s one reason NOSE calls every score a hypothesis rather than a prediction.
  • The jar keeps changing. Terpenes evaporate and break down after testing. One storage study measured average terpenoid losses of more than half within four months (Milay et al., 2020), so an old report can describe a louder jar than the one you open.

How NOSE shows both

  • Character is the fingerprint bar. Each segment’s width is an aroma family’s share of the measured total. The score itself compares compound by compound; the six families make the shape readable at a glance.
  • Intensity is the report’s total terpenes, shown on its own and never folded into the score.
  • Confidence tells you how many compounds the comparison rests on.

The math, including where it can be wrong, is on the methodology page.
